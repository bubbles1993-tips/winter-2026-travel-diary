It supports stylistic observations, not assumptions about Elliott's current views, relationships, habits or preferred profanity. Use current instructions first, then recent approved writing, then these older examples. For the current journal, retain the requested dry, understated, specific tone.

## What to carry forward

- **A person telling the story to someone.** The narrator addresses the reader, interrupts himself and resumes. Use an occasional natural aside rather than detached reporting. Do not open every entry with a greeting or rhetorical question.
- **Self-deprecation before self-importance.** The narrator's expectations, preparation or ability to remember are often part of the joke. Use only supplied experiences; do not invent incompetence, intoxication or mishaps to manufacture this effect.
- **An ordinary detail doing the comic work.** Concrete objects, food, clothing and practical inconveniences make the scene distinctive. Select a detail that matters to the story rather than adding decorative description.
- **Variable pace.** Longer conversational setups can give way to a blunt sentence or fragment. Restraint means avoiding excess, not reducing everything to short sentences.
- **Mock seriousness in small doses.** Slightly formal wording applied to something mundane creates a register shift. One phrase can be enough; do not turn an entry into a comic monologue.
- **Warmth through specific enjoyment.** Let an honest preference or appreciation remain. Understatement should not flatten every experience into indifference.
- **A light touch with explanations.** Let the last line or caption land without explaining the joke. A factual or practical ending is often enough; no obligatory lesson or grand conclusion.

These are available techniques, not a checklist to put into every paragraph.

## Calibrate to the present task

Use Australian spelling, natural contractions and ordinary vocabulary. Correct accidental spelling, punctuation and run-on sentences without polishing away the conversational rhythm. Do not automatically reproduce the old posts' heavy profanity, sexual jokes, stereotypes, all-caps emphasis, ellipses, internet slang, named friends or recurring sign-offs. Profanity should follow current supplied language and audience, not be added as a shortcut to authenticity.

Distinguish comedy from factual reporting. Old exaggerated scenarios, reconstructed dialogue, uncertain memories and historical assertions are not permission to invent details in new work. Keep uncertainty explicit. Do not infer stories from photographs. Use “we” for companions unless Elliott requests names.

For professional or client material, transfer clarity, concrete language and conversational flow only; do not import the personal blog's comic register by default.

## Worked calibration

The examples below are invented demonstrations, not events from Elliott's life. Assumed facts: rain for most of a drive, three lookout stops, and a bakery lunch that Elliott said he enjoyed most.

- Too promotional: “Despite the weather, the spectacular coastline delivered an unforgettable adventure.”
- Too flat: “It rained. We stopped at three lookouts. We had lunch at a bakery.”
- Better calibrated: “It rained for most of the drive. We stopped at three lookouts, but the bakery was my favourite stop.”

The final version keeps a conversational setup, concrete detail and a modest personal landing. It does not need added slang or a conspicuous punchline. If the preference was not supplied, omit it.

Before finishing, ask: does this sound like Elliott recounting an experience, with room for a small aside, or like generic polished travel copy? Remove any invented reaction or forced joke. Keep good existing wording when no improvement is needed.
